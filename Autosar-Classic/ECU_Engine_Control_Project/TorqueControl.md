The torque control function is a core component of the electric vehicle's control system. It regulates and manages the amount of torque the motor needs to provide to ensure the vehicle can operate safely, smoothly and efficiently. Torque determines the vehicle's ability to accelerate, decelerate and maintain speed. This control process depends on the driver's inputs (such as the accelerator pedal) and operating conditions (such as speed, load, road conditions).
**1.**Operating principle:
    - Receiving signals from the accelerator pedal sensor:
When the driver presses or releases the accelerator pedal, the accelerator pedal position sensor sends a signal to the ECU (Electronic Control Unit).
This signal tells the ECU how much the driver has pressed the accelerator (from 0% to 100%). For example, if the driver presses the accelerator hard, the signal value may be close to 100%, and if it is only lightly pressed, the value will be smaller.
    - Signal analysis and environmental factors:
The ECU not only uses information from the accelerator pedal but also analyzes other environmental factors such as:
Current vehicle speed: Provided by the speed sensor.
Vehicle load: Based on the sensor system or operating conditions.
Road conditions: Such as traction, slipperiness, etc., through systems such as Traction Control.
All this information is calculated by the ECU and determines the appropriate torque level to be supplied to the engine.
    - Determine the required torque:
Based on the driver's input and the environmental conditions, the ECU will calculate the torque level that the engine needs to produce.
For example, if the vehicle is running on a flat road at high speed, the required torque level may be lower when the vehicle is climbing a hill. Or when there is a large load, the ECU will request higher torque to maintain speed.
**2.**Torque Control:
    - Using PWM (Pulse Width Modulation):
The ECU controls torque using PWM, a method of signal modulation by varying the duty cycle of electrical pulses. PWM helps to smoothly regulate the amount of current supplied to the motor.
The PWM duty cycle (also known as duty cycle) is the percentage of time the signal is "on" during each cycle. The higher the duty cycle, the more power the motor receives, and therefore the greater the torque.
For example:
If the PWM duty cycle is 25%, the motor receives only 25% of its maximum power, producing low torque.
If the duty cycle is 75%, the motor receives 75% of its power, producing greater torque.
    - Torque Regulation Process:
Once the ECU determines the required torque level, it adjusts the PWM duty cycle to deliver the right amount of power to the engine.
This process is fast and continuous, allowing the vehicle to respond immediately to changes from the driver, such as when the throttle is pressed or released.
By adjusting the torque, the ECU ensures that the vehicle can accelerate smoothly when the driver presses the throttle, or decelerate slowly when the driver releases the throttle.
    - System Feedback:
        Torque Sensor:
            To ensure that the actual torque matches the required torque, a torque sensor is used to provide feedback to the ECU.
This sensor directly measures the torque generated by the engine and sends this data back to the ECU for comparison with the calculated target torque.
        Comparison and adjustment:
            If there is a deviation between the actual torque and the required torque, the ECU will adjust the PWM duty cycle.
For example, if the actual torque is lower than the required torque, the ECU will increase the PWM duty cycle to provide more power to the engine. If the actual torque is too high, the ECU will reduce the duty cycle to reduce the amount of power supplied to the engine.
This process operates according to the closed-loop control model, ensuring that the torque is always maintained at the exact level required.
**3.**Application conditions and effectiveness:
    - Acceleration: When the driver presses the accelerator pedal hard, the ECU will calculate the larger torque level and adjust the PWM cycle to provide more power to the engine. This helps the vehicle accelerate quickly.
    - Deceleration: When the driver releases the accelerator pedal, the torque level decreases, and the ECU adjusts the PWM cycle to reduce the amount of power supplied to the motor, allowing the vehicle to decelerate gradually.
    - Maintaining speed: The ECU can also adjust the torque to maintain a stable speed when the vehicle is running with a changing load or road conditions, such as when the vehicle is climbing or descending a hill.
    - Benefits of torque control:
Energy saving: By adjusting the torque precisely, the ECU helps optimize the amount of energy used, thereby improving the energy efficiency of the electric vehicle.
Improved driving experience: The system responds quickly to the driver's actions, providing precise control and enhancing the feeling of smooth and stable driving.
Safer: Torque control helps prevent wheel spin and maintain vehicle stability, especially in slippery road conditions or when carrying heavy loads.
