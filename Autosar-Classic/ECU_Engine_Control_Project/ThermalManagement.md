The engine temperature management function is an important component in protecting the engine from overheating, maintaining high performance and ensuring the life of the system. Excessive temperatures not only affect the performance of the engine but can also cause serious damage, so the thermal management system helps to regulate and maintain the temperature at a safe level throughout the operation of the vehicle.
**Operating principle:**
    - Continuous monitoring of engine temperature:
The temperature management system operates based on data from temperature sensors mounted on the engine and other important components such as the cooler or heat exchanger. This sensor continuously monitors the temperature of the engine and sends a signal to the ECU (Electronic Control Unit).
The ECU processes this temperature data to ensure that the engine is operating within safe temperature limits.
    - Safe temperature threshold:
The safe threshold is the maximum temperature at which the engine can operate without damage. This threshold can vary depending on the type of engine, but typically ranges from 80°C to 120°C for electric motors.
When the temperature exceeds this threshold, the engine is at risk of damage as internal mechanical and electronic components may be overloaded, worn out, or destroyed.
    - Activating the cooling system:
If the temperature exceeds a safe level, the ECU will activate the cooling systems to lower the engine temperature. Common cooling systems include:
Cooling fan: The fan is activated to increase air circulation around the engine, helping to cool it quickly.
Water pump: For liquid cooling systems, the water pump increases the flow rate of coolant through the engine to absorb and release heat.
Cooling system: The ECU can adjust the opening or closing of cooling valves or heat exchangers to increase the ability to dissipate heat.
**Engine Protection:**
    - Reduced Engine Power:
When the temperature exceeds a threshold and the cooling system cannot bring the temperature down fast enough, the ECU will take proactive protective measures by reducing engine power. This may include reducing the current supplied to the engine or reducing torque, which reduces the load on the engine and thus reduces heat generation.
For example, if the ECU detects a rapid increase in engine temperature under heavy load, it can limit speed or power to reduce heat generation.
    - Limp Mode:
If the temperature continues to rise and reaches a dangerous level, the ECU can activate a limp mode. In this mode, the engine will reduce power sharply or even stop running to protect against serious damage.
For example, if the temperature reaches 120°C, the engine can temporarily stop running to prevent damage to key components such as the windings or internal circuits of the engine.
    - System Feedback:
Temperature Sensor:
A temperature sensor mounted directly on the engine or cooling system continuously monitors the temperature. When this sensor detects that the temperature has increased beyond the specified level, it will send a signal to the ECU to activate the cooling system.
Temperature data from the sensor is continuously sent to the ECU, helping the system always have updated and accurate information to adjust the cooling strategy.
Closed-loop Feedback:
The temperature management system operates on the principle of closed-loop feedback, which means that the temperature sensors continuously send data about the current temperature of the engine to the ECU. The ECU will process this data and make a decision to adjust the cooling system or engine power to maintain a safe temperature.
If the temperature drops to a safe level after activating the cooling system, the ECU can reduce or turn off the cooling system to avoid wasting energy.
Continuous monitoring and flexible adjustment:
The ECU not only activates the cooling system when the temperature exceeds the threshold, but it also continuously monitors the engine temperature throughout the operation. Even when the engine is operating at light or medium load, if an abnormal increase in temperature is detected, the system will react quickly to prevent overheating from occurring.
**Main components of the thermal management system:**
    - Temperature Sensors:
Temperature sensors are indispensable components, often installed in important locations such as the engine case or cooling system. This sensor measures the temperature and sends a signal to the ECU.
    - Cooling Fans:
Cooling fans are the basic means of increasing air circulation around the engine, helping to cool the temperature quickly when necessary. The ECU controls the fans based on temperature data and only turns them on when needed to save energy.
    - Water Pump:
In liquid cooling systems, the water pump plays an important role in circulating coolant through the engine and radiator. As the temperature increases, the ECU will activate the water pump to increase the flow rate of coolant, helping to absorb heat more effectively.
    - Heat Exchangers:
This is the part that helps release heat from the coolant to the environment. The ECU can adjust the opening and closing of valves or flaps on the heat exchanger to control the airflow and optimize the heat dissipation process.
